# PulseSense ❤️
### Understanding, Simulating and Visualizing the Electrical Activity of the Human Heart

PulseSense is a heart monitoring and ECG visualization project developed using Python. It explores how the human heart generates electrical impulses, how these signals can be acquired through sensors and microcontrollers, and how Python can be used to process and visualize them.

The project combines concepts from **human physiology, biomedical instrumentation, microcontrollers, signal processing, and Python programming** to understand the journey of a heartbeat from the human body to a computer screen.

---

## 1. Understanding the Human Heart

The human heart is a muscular organ that continuously pumps blood throughout the body. Its contractions are controlled by an internal electrical conduction system.

The heartbeat does not occur randomly. It is initiated and coordinated by electrical impulses generated within the heart.

### The Electrical Conduction System

The major components involved are:

- **SA Node (Sinoatrial Node):** The natural pacemaker of the heart. It generates electrical impulses that initiate each heartbeat.
- **AV Node (Atrioventricular Node):** Receives the impulse from the SA node and briefly delays it, allowing the atria to contract before the ventricles.
- **Bundle of His:** Conducts the electrical impulse from the AV node towards the ventricles.
- **Right and Left Bundle Branches:** Carry the impulse towards the respective ventricles.
- **Purkinje Fibres:** Distribute the electrical impulse throughout the ventricular muscles, producing coordinated ventricular contraction.

### How a Heartbeat Happens

1. The SA node generates an electrical impulse.
2. The impulse spreads across the atria, causing atrial contraction.
3. The signal reaches the AV node, where it is briefly delayed.
4. The impulse travels through the Bundle of His and bundle branches.
5. Purkinje fibres distribute the impulse throughout the ventricles.
6. The ventricles contract and pump blood to the lungs and the rest of the body.

## 2. Understanding the ECG Signal

An Electrocardiogram (ECG) is a graphical representation of the electrical activity of the heart recorded over time.

Each heartbeat produces a characteristic waveform.

### Main Components of an ECG

| Component | Physiological significance |
|---|---|
| P Wave | Atrial depolarization |
| QRS Complex | Ventricular depolarization |
| T Wave | Ventricular repolarization |
| PR Interval | Time taken for the impulse to travel from the atria towards the ventricles |
| ST Segment | Period between ventricular depolarization and repolarization |

### Calculating Heart Rate

Heart rate can be calculated using the time interval between two consecutive R-peaks.

**Formula:**

Heart Rate (BPM) = 60 / R-R Interval (seconds)

For example, if the R-R interval is 0.8 seconds:

Heart Rate = 60 / 0.8 = 75 BPM.

## 3. How Sensors Capture Heart Signals

The electrical activity generated by the heart can be detected using electrodes placed on the body's surface.

An ECG acquisition system generally consists of:

- **Electrodes:** Detect small electrical potential differences produced by the heart.
- **ECG Sensor / Analog Front-End:** Amplifies and conditions the weak ECG signal while helping reduce unwanted noise.
- **ADC (Analog-to-Digital Converter):** Converts the analog signal into digital samples.
- **Microcontroller:** Receives and processes the digitized signal.

The electrodes do not directly measure the mechanical heartbeat. They detect the electrical activity associated with cardiac function.

## 4. ESP32: Connecting Electronics with Programming

The ESP32 is a microcontroller that can be used to acquire and transmit ECG sensor data to a computer.

### Working Flow

1. ECG electrodes detect the electrical potential differences.
2. The ECG sensor conditions and amplifies the signal.
3. The ESP32 receives the analog signal through its ADC, depending on the sensor interface.
4. The microcontroller sends the sampled data to a computer through a communication interface such as USB Serial or Wi-Fi.
5. Python receives the incoming data.
6. The signal is processed and displayed as an ECG waveform.

**Important:** The current software prototype focuses on ECG simulation and dataset-based visualization. ESP32 and physical sensor integration represent the hardware direction for the project, not an already completed hardware implementation.

The general communication process is:

Python does not directly detect the heartbeat. It works with the electrical signal measured by the sensor and transmitted by the microcontroller.

## 6. ECG Signal Simulation Using the Gaussian Function

Before working with physical ECG hardware, it is useful to understand how an ECG waveform is formed mathematically.

PulseSense uses Gaussian functions to simulate the different components of an ECG waveform.

The Gaussian function is:

f(t) = A × exp(-((t - μ)² / (2σ²)))

Where:

- **A:** Amplitude of the waveform.
- **t:** Time variable.
- **μ (Mu):** Centre or position of the waveform.
- **σ (Sigma):** Controls the width or spread of the waveform.
- **exp:** Exponential function.

### Why Use Gaussian Functions?

A Gaussian function creates a smooth, bell-shaped curve.

By combining multiple Gaussian functions with different amplitudes, positions, widths, and signs, we can approximate the different waves of an ECG signal.

For example:

- P wave: Positive Gaussian component.
- Q wave: Negative Gaussian component.
- R wave: High positive Gaussian component.
- S wave: Negative Gaussian component.
- T wave: Positive Gaussian component.

The combined mathematical model produces a simulated ECG waveform.

**Note:** This is an educational approximation of an ECG signal. Real ECG waveforms have more complex morphology and cannot be fully represented by a simple combination of Gaussian functions.

## 7. Working with ECG Datasets

Simulated signals help us understand waveform generation, but real ECG datasets allow us to explore signals recorded from actual human subjects.

PulseSense uses ECG datasets to visualize real recorded cardiac signals.

### Dataset Workflow

1. Obtain an ECG dataset from a reliable source.
2. Download the dataset to the computer.
3. Load the dataset into Python.
4. Read the signal samples and their sampling frequency.
5. Convert sample indices into time values.
6. Plot the ECG waveform.
7. Analyze the waveform and identify important features such as R-peaks.

### Simulated ECG vs Dataset ECG

| Simulated ECG | Dataset ECG |
|---|---|
| Mathematically generated | Recorded from real subjects |
| Helps understand waveform formation | Helps explore real cardiac signals |
| Parameters can be controlled | Signal morphology varies |
| Useful for learning and testing | Useful for practical signal analysis |

A dataset is not a live heartbeat. It contains previously recorded measurements that can be loaded and analyzed.
